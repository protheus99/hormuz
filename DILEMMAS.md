# Ethical dilemmas — the deck

Twenty-eight scenarios for §12A.6, drafted 2026-09-22 for the owner to cut, keep and add to. None is
built yet.

**The rules they all follow** (§12A.6):

- **No meters.** Every other card shows Cash, Profit, Supply and Risk. These show none, because four
  numbers would decide the matter. They state what is certain and say plainly that the rest cannot be
  projected.
- **The payoff is Enron-shaped.** Cutting the corner usually pays, sometimes for years. What builds
  is hidden **exposure**, which never washes off, costs a little every day, and buys a reckoning that
  scales with everything taken.
- **Told plainly.** No dramatisation, no gore, no lecture. A death is reported as a death.
- **Rare.** Never two in quick succession. A deck this size is a career's worth, not a year's.

**On the source.** The owner suggested *Landman* as a well of examples. What is drawn from it is
thematic — fatal accidents and how the families are treated, undocumented labour, landowner
pressure, spills, the quick settlement before the lawyers arrive, the distance between the schedule
and doing the job properly. The situations below are written for this game's world and mechanics, not
lifted from its plot, which matters both legally and because our world is global with three play
types where the show is one company in West Texas.

**Reading the table.** *Certain* is what the card may state as fact. *Hidden* is what the engine
decides in secret and the player may never learn. *Exposure* is what the wrong answer adds to the
record.

## A. The people who work for you

**1. The manager's hunch** · producer
A maintenance manager wants wells pulled for a service early — he cannot say why, only that he does
not like what he is seeing.
*Yes* pull them now · *Maybe* the two he is most worried about · *No* carry on.
**Certain:** what the downtime costs. **Hidden:** whether he was right at all — the engine decides
that in secret, unconnected to the wells' true state, so the board cannot be read for the answer.
**Exposure:** on *No*, if he was right.

**2. The double shift** · producer, refiner
A crew has been on doubles for nine days to finish a workover. The foreman wants them stood down.
*Yes* stand them down · *Maybe* finish the shift, then rest · *No* push through.
**Certain:** two days of lost work. **Hidden:** whether tiredness costs someone a hand.

**3. The fire nobody reported** · producer, refiner
A small fire was put out in twenty minutes. Reporting it brings an inspection and a probable shutdown.
*Yes* report it · *Maybe* log it internally · *No* it never happened.
**Certain:** inspection and downtime if reported. **Hidden:** whether it surfaces later, and how much
worse it looks then.

**4. A man without papers** · producer
Your best crew chief has no right to work here. He has been with you four years.
*Yes* sponsor him properly · *Maybe* move him off the books · *No* let him go.
**Certain:** sponsorship costs money and takes months; letting him go slows every workover.
**Hidden:** whether an inspection ever asks.

**5. The detectors** · producer, refiner
A third of the gas detectors on site fail their test. A full replacement is expensive.
*Yes* replace them all · *Maybe* the worst of them · *No* recalibrate and carry on.
**Hidden:** whether the ones you skipped were the ones that mattered.

**6. The widow's settlement** · producer, refiner
After a death on site, your lawyers suggest offering the family a figure this week, before they find
counsel of their own.
*Yes* offer what a court would award · *Maybe* the lawyers' figure · *No* let them come to you.
**Certain:** the honest figure costs more today. **Hidden:** what it costs in three years.

**7. The safety numbers** · all
Your recordable-injury rate sets your insurance and your standing with partners. Two incidents could
be classed as first aid.
*Yes* report them as they were · *No* class them as first aid.
**Exposure:** immediate and quiet. **Hidden:** whether anyone ever reconciles the two records.

**8. The young hand** · producer
A seventeen-year-old lied about his age to get hired. He is good, and he needs the work.
*Yes* let him go · *Maybe* move him to the yard · *No* say nothing.

**9. The contractor's people** · all
Your contractor pays its crews late and houses them badly. It is cheaper than the alternative and it
is not your company.
*Yes* change contractor · *Maybe* demand terms and audit them · *No* it is not your business.

## B. The ground and the people on it

**10. The creek** · producer
A leak reached water on the edge of the lease. It is small.
*Yes* report and clean it · *Maybe* clean it quietly · *No* wait for the rain.
**Certain:** reporting means a shutdown. **Hidden:** whether the rancher downstream notices first.

**11. The rancher's water** · producer
The landowner says your water use has drawn his well down. He may be right.
*Yes* pay what he asks · *Maybe* a token and a new pump · *No* let him sue.

**12. The flare** · producer
You are flaring above what the permit allows. Fixing it means shutting in for a fortnight.
*Yes* shut in and fix it · *Maybe* cut the rate to legal levels · *No* keep flaring.

**13. The wells that came with the lease** · producer
The block you bought has eleven old wells on it, unplugged, that nobody has been responsible for in
thirty years.
*Yes* plug them all · *Maybe* the two that are leaking · *No* they are not yours.
*Plugs into the lease system: this is the abandonment liability deferred from §12A.8.*

**14. The road** · producer, trader
Your trucks have broken up the road through a village. The council has written twice.
*Yes* rebuild it · *Maybe* pay the council and let them · *No* reroute the trucks at night.

**15. What the excavator found** · producer
Site work turned up something old. Reporting it stops work for months.
*Yes* report it · *No* the machine was already moving.

## C. The books

**16. The quarter that nearly worked** · all
You are a fortnight from the end and just short of the number. A cargo still at sea could be booked
as sold today.
*Yes* book it when it lands · *No* book it now.
*Plugs into the campaign: the temptation only appears when a goal is genuinely within reach.*

**17. The reserves report** · producer
Your engineer's estimate for a lease is well below last year's published figure. The bank lends
against that figure.
*Yes* publish his number · *Maybe* publish a range · *No* republish last year's.
**Mechanical:** the credit line follows the published figure, so the lie pays immediately.

**18. The insurance claim** · producer, refiner
The damage was real. That it followed a service you deferred is also real.
*Yes* claim honestly · *Maybe* leave the maintenance record out · *No* claim the lot.

**19. The auditor's error** · all
An auditor has made a mistake in your favour, worth a good deal.
*Yes* tell them · *No* sign it.

**20. Ninety days** · all
A small supplier is owed. Paying at ninety days instead of thirty would carry you through a tight
month; it may not carry them.
*Yes* pay on time · *Maybe* pay half · *No* take the ninety days.

**21. The cargo that is two per cent short** · trader
The buyer's surveyor is not thorough. Your cargo is short and he has not noticed.
*Yes* tell him · *No* let it go.

**22. The paperwork that does not add up** · trader, refiner
A cargo is offered below the market. Its papers say one origin; its history suggests another you may
not legally buy from.
*Yes* refuse it · *Maybe* demand the chain of title · *No* take it and do not ask.

## D. Information and influence

**23. The informant** · all
Someone from a rival's operation offers to tell you what they are doing, for a price.
*Yes* pay · *No* decline.
**Hidden:** whether the information is any good, and whether the rival finds out.

**24. The seismic data** · producer
A contractor who shot a survey for a rival will sell you a copy before the auction.
*Yes* buy it · *No* bid on the published survey like everyone else.
**Mechanical:** it reveals a lot's true band. *Plugs straight into the auction — the strongest fit in
the deck, and the one to build first.*

**25. The number you were not meant to hear** · producer
The night before the sale, someone tells you what a rival intends to bid.
*Yes* use it · *No* bid as you were going to.
**Mechanical:** it reveals a rival's sealed bid.

**26. A friend at the ministry** · producer
A licence in a region that wants one could be had in weeks rather than a year, for a consultancy fee
to the right firm.
*Yes* pay it · *No* wait your turn.
**Mechanical:** skips the licence cost and delay.

**27. The berth** · trader
Your cargo is fourth in the queue. A payment to the right official makes it first.
*Yes* pay · *No* wait.

**28. The story about someone else** · all
A journalist is writing about your safety record. Your advisers suggest giving her a better story
about a competitor.
*Yes* give her the story · *No* answer her questions.

## What to build first

Six of these need no new systems and plug into machinery that already exists — the auction, the
licence, the lease, the credit line, the wells:

**24** seismic data, **25** the overheard bid, **26** the friend at the ministry, **13** the orphan
wells, **17** the reserves report, **1** the manager's hunch.

Start there, see whether the shape works in play, and then the other twenty-two are mostly text.

## What is still open

- **How many in a career.** Twenty-eight is a deck, not a schedule. At one every two or three months
  a three-year game sees a dozen.
- **Whether refusing should ever cost.** Every card above is safe to refuse. A deck where virtue is
  always free teaches its own lesson; some of these could carry a real price for the honest answer —
  6, 11, 20 and 26 are the candidates.
- **Whether the player ever learns.** Most of these hide their outcome for ever. A few could pay off
  visibly years later, which is what makes the rest feel dangerous.
